Activity 01

Spot the AI: Workplace Email Patterns

Analyze everyday workplace emails to identify robotic language patterns versus authentic human operational leadership.

Task Overview & Instructions

Large Language Models (LLMs) are probabilistic prediction engines, not thinkers[cite: 8]. When given vague or unrefined prompts, they often produce stilted, overly formal prose and jargon-heavy sentences[cite: 7, 14]. As a team leader, you must be able to spot these hallmarks to ensure organizational communications remain clear, human, and grounded[cite: 7, 9].

What you must do:
  1. Review each of the 4 workplace scenarios below[cite: 7].
  2. Compare Email A and Email B side-by-side[cite: 7].
  3. Click the button indicating which draft was generated by AI[cite: 7].
  4. Read the feedback box to understand the specific linguistic giveaways and leadership takeaways[cite: 7, 9].
Case 1: Payroll & Mileage Submission Cutoff[cite: 7]
Email A

"Hi Team, just a quick reminder that all mileage claims must be submitted by Friday at 4 PM to ensure processing for this month's payroll cycle. No late exceptions can be made. Thanks!"[cite: 7]

Email B

"Greetings valued colleagues! Please be cordially reminded that all vehicle transit reimbursement documentation must be filed prior to the conclusion of Friday's operational hours."[cite: 7]

Which email was generated by AI?[cite: 7]

Incorrect: Email A uses authentic, direct human cadence and clear business operational expectations[cite: 7].
Correct! Email B features classic unrefined LLM hallmarks: unnatural over-formality ("cordially reminded", "vehicle transit reimbursement documentation") and passive syntax[cite: 7, 8].
Case 2: Sickness & Absence Reporting Procedure
Email A

"Morning all, if you're feeling unwell and unable to work today, please give me a direct ring on my mobile before 8:30 AM rather than pinging WhatsApp or Slack. It helps us organize shift cover smoothly."

Email B

"In the unexpected event that personal wellness parameters inhibit your ability to execute assigned daily responsibilities, prompt verbal synchronization via telephonic apparatus is requested by 08:30 hours."

Which email was generated by AI?[cite: 7]

Incorrect: Email A represents normal, empathetic team supervisory communication.
Correct! "Personal wellness parameters" and "telephonic apparatus" are overt robotic hallucinations of formal business communication[cite: 8].
Case 3: Critical IT CRM Outage Alert
Email A

"URGENT NOTICE: It has come to institutional attention that our client relation infrastructure is experiencing non-nominal uptime friction. Stakeholders must refrain from data input initiatives pending holistic remediation."

Email B

"Team: The CRM is currently down nationwide. IT is actively working on it. Please record any new call notes in a local notepad document for now so we don't lose client records. I will update you by 11:00 AM."

Which email was generated by AI?[cite: 7]

Correct! Email A is bloated with AI jargon ("non-nominal uptime friction", "data input initiatives") that obscures critical operational instructions[cite: 8].
Incorrect: Email B is practical crisis leadership: direct problem statement, clear workaround, and scheduled update.
Case 4: Celebrating Quarter-End Target Success
Email A

"Massive shout-out to everyone for smashing our Q3 audit score today! Your hard work over the last three weeks made all the difference. Coffee and pastries on me during tomorrow's morning huddle!"

Email B

"I hereby extend profound gratitude for your exemplary adherence to operational metrics culminating in exceptional third-quarter outcomes. Let us celebrate our symbiotic achievements during future scheduled interactions."

Which email was generated by AI?[cite: 7]

Incorrect: Email A shows genuine human warmth, team recognition, and specific celebration details.
Correct! Email B is sterile and artificial ("symbiotic achievements", "profound gratitude for your exemplary adherence") lacking authentic emotional intelligence[cite: 8].

Human-in-the-Loop: As a team leader, you are personally accountable for the tone and clarity of communications sent to your team[cite: 9, 16].
