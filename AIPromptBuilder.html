Activity 03 • 15-Minute Leadership Lab

Team Leader Prompt Architect: RTC Masterclass

Master the Role–Task–Context (RTC) model: diagnose flawed workplace prompts and build calibrated, balanced prompts that drive team compliance without destroying trust.

Lab Overview & Operational Flow

Unrefined prompts force AI prediction models to guess operational scope and tone, triggering defensive, aggressive, or corporate-bloated outputs. Team leaders must know how to spot prompt flaws and construct structured prompts that protect psychological safety while streamlining workflows.

Phase 1

Workplace Prompt Diagnostic: Good vs. Bad

Before writing your own prompt, evaluate these 4 common prompts written by managers. Determine whether each prompt is an Effective RTC Prompt or a Flawed Prompt, and inspect the operational consequence.

Case A: Handover Update

"Write a handover note for the night shift."

Case B: Shift Lateness Notice

"Write an email to a team member who was 20 minutes late twice this week telling them this is unacceptable, threatens their job, and will lead to an immediate disciplinary hearing."

Case C: Process Change Briefing

"Act as an experienced operational team supervisor. Draft a 3-bullet point morning huddle script explaining our switch from paper defect logs to the Bay 2 tablet app. Keep the tone encouraging, emphasize that 5-minute practice sessions are scheduled during shift change, and limit the response to 90 words."

Case D: Client Meeting Summary

"Summarize this client dispute transcript, make our logistics subcontractor look completely at fault so we don't have to refund the customer, and make it sound extremely legalistic."

Phase 2

Active Challenge: Build the Calibrated Leadership Prompt

The Operational Dilemma

A manager entered this lazy prompt: "Write a message telling my team to stop leaving dirty coffee mugs in the sink." The AI generated a hostile, four-paragraph disciplinary threat citing surveillance and misconduct, which outraged the team. Your mission is to re-architect this prompt using the RTC Formula to drive compliance while maintaining a positive, high-trust culture.

R

Role

Instruct the AI to adopt the persona of an approachable, supportive team leader or supervisor.

T

Task

Specify the exact deliverable: a brief team channel reminder (Slack/Teams) about rinsing and loading mugs into the dishwasher.

C

Context & Constraints

Enforce guardrails: a warm, lighthearted tone, a word/sentence limit (e.g. under 60 words or 3 sentences), and no threats or blame.

Evaluation Result

    Model Benchmark Prompt

    "Act as an approachable, supportive operational team leader. Draft a concise 3-sentence team channel reminder (Slack/Teams) asking the team to rinse and load their mugs into the dishwasher. Keep the tone warm, lighthearted, and focused on collective pride in our shared space, with a maximum length of 60 words."

    Notice how explicit boundaries prevent the AI from generating defensive rhetoric or formal reprimands.

    Human-in-the-Loop Principle: As a leader, you are personally accountable for any AI-assisted output and the workplace culture it creates.
