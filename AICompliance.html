Activity 02

AI Compliance & Governance Gatekeeper

Protect organizational assets, customer trust, and sensitive data under GDPR and corporate Acceptable Use Policies.

Task Overview & Instructions

Anything entered into public generative AI tools is stored on external cloud servers and can be used to train future public models. As an operational leader covering apprenticeship standards K5 (Legislation & Compliance) and K10 (Policies & Culture), you are the operational firewall. You must distinguish between safe automation workflows and critical corporate data breaches.

What you must do:
  1. Review each of the 6 operational workplace scenarios below.
  2. Evaluate whether the proposed action complies with data governance guidelines or exposes sensitive data.
  3. Select either Safe Application or Data Breach for each item.
  4. Read the feedback explanations to reinforce corporate compliance rules regarding PII, Special Category Data, and shadow AI extensions.
Scenario 1: Operational Improvement

You paste a list of 10 customer service complaints with names, ticket IDs, and delivery addresses completely stripped out (e.g., "Delivery arrived 2 days late", "Item crushed in transit") into a public LLM to cluster recurring root-cause themes.

Safe: Full anonymization ensures zero Personally Identifiable Information (PII) is uploaded. This safely automates operational root-cause analysis.
Incorrect: Because identifiers and customer traces are entirely stripped, analyzing general phrasing complies with GDPR principles.
Scenario 2: Departmental Budgeting

To prepare for your quarterly executive meeting, you upload your operational unit's actual monthly financial expenditure spreadsheet into an open web AI tool to extract variance bullet points.

Critical Breach: Public models use inputs to train future models. Exposing actual business expenditure, profit margins, or cost codes violates organizational governance and commercial confidentiality.
Correct (Critical Breach): Internal corporate financials and strategy documents must never be entered into public tools without enterprise zero-retention infrastructure.
Scenario 3: People Management & Appraisals

You want to speed up writing an annual performance review. You paste an apprentice's full name, attendance percentage, disciplinary warnings, and client feedback into a free AI chatbot to draft their appraisal narrative.

Major Breach: Employee performance records and disciplinary status are confidential personal data. Uploading them to a third-party engine violates employee trust and data protection regulations.
Correct (Major Breach): Team leader appraisal duties require strict confidentiality. Identifiable personnel records must never be exposed to public web scrapers or cloud models.
Scenario 4: Sickness & Health Summaries

A team member submits a doctor's medical note with complex clinical terminology. You paste the diagnostic notes into an AI tool to simplify the condition into a return-to-work adjustments plan.

Critical Breach (Special Category Data): Health data is Special Category PII under GDPR. It requires the highest tier of security. Pasting medical notes into commercial AI is a severe breach.
Correct (Critical Breach): Health and medical details are Special Category Data. Entering clinical diagnostics into public tools exposes sensitive personal data to external third parties.
Scenario 5: Routine Handover Formatting

You draft a shift handover summary without colleague names or machine serial codes: "Morning line hit 94% target. Bay 2 scheduled for maintenance at 14:00. Safety checks clear." You paste it in to turn it into professional bullet points.

Safe: There are no personal details, trade secrets, or client names. Refining routine operational prose for clarity is an approved, safe efficiency use case.
Incorrect: When sensitive identifiers are absent, basic syntax formatting is a safe and encouraged use of generative tools.
Scenario 6: Tool Integration & Governance

A team member finds a free browser extension that automatically transcribes and summarizes all internal Microsoft Teams meetings, and installs it onto company laptops without consulting the IT or Information Governance team.

Governance Breach: "Shadow AI" installations create silent data leaks. Unvetted third-party browser extensions often harvest meeting audio and internal discussions back to external servers.
Correct (Governance Breach): As a team leader, you must enforce company Acceptable Use Policies. Any AI tool processing internal voice or video requires formal Information Security vetting.

Operational Principle: Never enter customer PII, staff records, or internal company financials into unvetted public models.
